# Sprint UI-7 — Analytics

**Date:** 2026-08-15 · **Status:** Complete · **Follows:** `docs/sprint-ui-6-nfc-qr.md`

Analytics could previously answer "how many" over a fixed 30-day window and nothing else.
With `source` and `tag_id` now captured (UI-6), it can answer *how people arrived*, *which
card*, *where*, *when*, and — the one that matters most — *what we can actually prove
happened*.

---

## The mandatory distinction, implemented

CLAUDE.md §15: **CLICK and CONVERSION must be distinguished.** `lib/analytics.ts` makes that
a rule in code rather than a caption:

| Class | Events | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Reach** | tap, scan, view | someone arrived |
| **Engagement** | click | someone pressed something and **left our origin** |
| **Confirmed** | download, lead | completed **inside our own code** |

Only the third is a conversion in any defensible sense. A click on a Google review button
tells us the link was followed and nothing about whether a review was written — the browser
leaves and never reports back. The vCard is generated by our code and the lead is written by
our server, so those two we can stand behind.

The UI states this in plain words rather than burying it: *"a review click is not a review,
and a WhatsApp click is not a message."*

### The denominator problem, handled honestly

A rate needs a denominator, and the obvious one is wrong. An NFC tap on a smart page records
**both** a `tap` (at `/t/<token>`) and a `view` (when the page renders), so "clicks ÷ all
arrivals" would divide by a number that counts one visit twice. Redirect links record a tap
with no page to click on at all.

`pageOpens()` is therefore **views + QR scans** — the set of visits that could possibly have
produced a click — and the UI says so under the number. Rates return `undefined` rather than
0% when there is nothing to divide by, the same rule as `percentChange`.

## What shipped

### Migration `0011` — one analytics RPC

`get_analytics(p_days, p_page_id)` serves both the account report and the per-profile view;
pass a page id to scope it, or null for everything. Two near-identical functions would drift,
and the per-page view had already fallen a sprint behind the dashboard. Returns totals, the
prior window, a zero-filled daily series, and breakdowns by **source**, **device**,
**country**, **hour of day**, action and profile.

`get_page_analytics` is superseded but **not dropped** (§19.7).

### Both analytics screens, from one component

`AnalyticsReport` is shared, so the account view and the per-profile view cannot diverge
again. Both gained the 7/30/90 range selector the per-page view never had.

### Breakdowns

- **How visitors arrived** — NFC / QR / Direct. Events predating UI-6 have no captured source
  and are labelled **"Not recorded"**, never folded into Direct, which would silently
  overstate that channel.
- **Which card** — per-device taps, the payoff from `events.tag_id`.
- **Where** — country from network location, resolved through `Intl.DisplayNames`, so no
  country-name dependency. Labelled approximate, because it is.
- **When** — hour of day in **East Africa Time**. UTC hours would be three hours off and
  actively misleading for "when do customers tap". Kenya is the launch market (§22); per-account
  time zones arrive with expansion.

### `busiestWindow()` reports a fact, not advice

It returns the busiest three-hour stretch and its share — *"38% of activity happens between
2 PM and 5 PM"*. It deliberately stops short of the mockup's *"ask for reviews at 2–4pm"*:
that is an inference about customer psychology the data does not support. If it belongs
anywhere it is UI-10, with reasoning attached.

### CSV export

Daily aggregates, not raw events: an event-level dump carries per-visitor rows into a
spreadsheet for no gain the daily breakdown does not already provide, and widens the blast
radius of a shared file. Scoped by RLS through the RPC, so a page id in the query string
cannot reach another account. `no-store, private` — this is business performance data.
The confirmed-rate column is **blank**, not `0`, when there were no opens: `0%` in a
spreadsheet reads as a measured result.

## Technical debt

- Hour-of-day is hard-coded to `Africa/Nairobi`. Correct for the launch market and clearly
  labelled, but it becomes wrong the moment a customer is outside East Africa. A per-account
  time zone belongs with the first non-Kenyan market.
- Country data comes from `x-vercel-ip-country`, so it is only populated in production behind
  Vercel — locally it is always null.
- `lib/metrics.ts` and `lib/analytics.ts` both hold event vocabulary. They serve different
  jobs (dashboard summary vs analytical classification) but the overlap should be watched.
- Carried forward: no error/loading boundaries, `force-dynamic` everywhere, no `public/` or
  favicon, subscription expiry unenforced (B13), `middleware`→`proxy` rename pending, the
  builder still unexercised by hand, and **physical NFC verification still outstanding from
  UI-6**.

## Next sprint proposal — UI-8: Leads / Customers

The lead inbox is still a list with contact links. UI-8 makes it a workflow: a detail view,
status or stage so a lead can be worked rather than just read, notes, filtering by profile
and date, search, and mobile-first cards. Likely backend: a `status` column on `leads` plus
an optional note — small, and the first schema change driven purely by workflow rather than
by reporting. Worth deciding then whether "Customers" should mean *people* (deduplicated
across submissions) rather than *submissions*, which is the real difference between a lead
inbox and a CRM.
